The incident board for the command post.

KP Rück turns the magnetic board in the rear command post into a digital one: incidents, crews, vehicles and equipment on one board – live on every device.

Grown out of the real operational work of Feuerwehr Oberwil BL – proven there, and developed further ever since.

Three layers of redundancy.

The benchmark

«When the network is gone, paper is in charge»

A board that goes black in a power cut is not a command tool. So redundancy is built in at several levels – every level works without the one above it.

Level 1

Several devices, always in sync

Tablets, projector displays and phones all hang live off the same board. If one device fails or freezes, another carries on – there is nothing to rescue and nothing to catch up on.

Level 2

A standby box at the station

A mini PC at the station mirrors the board continuously. If the internet goes down, it takes over: the board keeps running on the local network, with the map from your own tile server and the printer on the LAN. Once the network is back, what was run there is synced again.

Level 3

Paper is in charge

The state of the board goes to the thermal printer every few minutes, the situation sheet at the push of a button. If everything fails, the last sheet becomes the board. On restart, one person transfers what was done by hand back into the app.

Magnetic board on the wall

One board on every device.

Columns are incident phases: cards move from left to right by drag and drop. The projector in the command post, the tablet on the table and the quartermaster’s phone all show the same state – without anybody pressing «refresh».

  • Assign personnel, vehicles and equipment – KP Rück warns about double assignments
  • Whoever arrives checks themselves in with a QR code; duty times and breaks are tracked
  • Assign by typing: «14 tlf meier» in the command palette

Telephone notes on slips of paper

Reports attached to the incident.

Through the reporting form, by telephone, or over the open interface from the dispatch system (Divera or FireHub, for example). The duty officer takes one over with a click – the report becomes an incident on the board.

  • Reporting form by link or QR for telephone callers and walk-ins, without an account
  • If someone reports the same incident site a second time, the board asks
  • Open interface – any dispatch system can open incidents

Pins on the town map

Who is where.

Every incident on one map, with the vehicles optionally by GPS position. When a vehicle reaches the scene, the board proposes the status change itself.

  • Every incident by priority and status on one map
  • Assignments as a line – which vehicle belongs to which incident
  • Precipitation radar and official warnings (MeteoSwiss, Alertswiss)

WhatsApp group and radio message

The team reports from its phone.

Every team sees its own incident sites: arrived, pickup, incident finished – without the radio. The recon team fills in its form with photos on its own phone; the report hangs on the right incident instead of getting lost in a group chat.

  • Field view without an account, by link and code
  • Requests – equipment, reinforcements, pickup – stay open until the command post has handled them
  • Assignments: several incident sites as one route, the order optimised by distance

Report the weekend after

The incident report writes itself along the way.

The incident log and the response times build up as you go and turn into a PDF. Corrections are made with new entries, not with an eraser – so the night stays on the record.

  • The log records status changes, assignments and messages by itself
  • Incident report as a PDF with the response times
  • Outstanding-items list: open reports, pickups and equipment still at the scene

Running it

Your data stays with you.

KP Rück is open source and runs on a machine in your own station or in your own cloud. Incidents, crews, vehicles and equipment stay under your control. No licence fees, no vendor lock-in.

Questions & answers

The most common questions.

Why switch?

No more double bookings. No report lost in a group chat. Less radio traffic for questions that are on the board anyway. Everybody sees the same picture at the same time – and the incident report is already there when you get back, instead of the weekend after.

Which fire brigades and incidents is KP Rück for?

For militia, local, regional support and professional fire brigades – for everyday incidents, storm situations and several events at once. Wherever crews, vehicles and equipment have to be coordinated behind the lines, KP Rück plays to its strengths. As open-source software it can also be adapted for civil protection, command staffs and other emergency response organisations.

Where does the data live, and which interfaces are there?

Like KP Front, KP Rück is entirely self-hosted – on a machine at the station or in the organisation’s own cloud. Incidents, crews, vehicles and equipment live in its own database; there is no licence check and no usage tracking. External services such as online maps or address search receive only what the feature in use needs, and can be switched off. You can connect alerting systems (any dispatch system through the open interface, Divera and FireHub directly – with Divera, crew alerting too), a thermal printer on the local network and vehicle GPS positions. Every integration is optional.

What does it cost?

The software itself costs nothing. KP Rück is open source (AGPL) – no licence, no subscription, no per-device fee. What remains are your own costs for operation, infrastructure and any external support you may need.

What does it take to run?

A small mini-PC or server at the station is enough. Setup, updates and backups require someone with Docker and command-line experience, whether in the brigade, the municipality or an IT support provider. The guide and administration area lead through the setup. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.

Is support available for installation and operation?

Yes. General questions about installation and use can be answered free of charge. KP Rück can be operated on an organisation’s own infrastructure or with a hosting or cloud provider of its choice. Support with installation, configuration, updates or operation is also available by individual agreement. More extensive support may be subject to a fee.

What happens during a power, network or device failure?

KP Rück saves the current state within the application and can restore it after a failure. The application can be operated on several devices and on an additional local instance. The current board can also be printed regularly and used as a fallback. External backups of the installation and infrastructure are the responsibility of the operating organisation.

Does KP Rück require KP Front?

No. KP Rück runs entirely on its own – with its own database, its own login and its own updates. It comes into its own alongside KP Front, the application for the front line at the incident: both take the same alarms from the dispatch system, read the same roster and classify alarms by the same keyword list, optionally with the same Divera access. Front and rear thus work from the same source data, without maintaining the alarm integration and the crew twice.

What is the local station printer for in KP Rück?

The station printer puts what matters on paper, right at the station or in the command post. KP Rück prints assignment slips for teams and drivers automatically if you wish, as soon as an incident is dispatched for the first time, or by hand at any time; the situation overview with incidents, vehicle status and crews, and the pickup list of equipment still on site, at the push of a button. As a fallback, the board can also be printed at regular intervals. What you connect is an ordinary 80 mm thermal printer (ESC/POS) on the local network – it works for any fire brigade, not just in Switzerland.

Can we adapt it to how we work?

Yes. Every brigade has its own administration area: incident types, priorities, crew, fleet and equipment lists are all configurable there, and import and export run through Excel. And because the whole code is on GitHub , everything else can be built – by you or to order.

Which languages are available for KP Rück?

The KP Rück user interface is currently available in German and French. As an open-source application, KP Rück can be extended to further languages. This requires a complete translation of the terminology and a professional review.

How do we start?

With the demo, without an account – pick a role and go. Then try it on an exercise; exercise mode is cleanly separated from the real thing. If it fits, the setup guide leads through installation and operation. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.
