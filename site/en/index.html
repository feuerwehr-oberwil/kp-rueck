The benchmark

«When the network is gone, paper is in charge»

A board that goes black in a power cut is not a command tool. So redundancy is built in at several levels – every level works without the one above it.

Level 1

Several devices on the same state

Tablets, projector displays and phones all hang live off the same board. If one device fails or freezes, another carries on – there is nothing to rescue and nothing to catch up on.

Level 2

A second instance at the station

KP Rück can also be run locally alongside. The built-in sync keeps events, incidents, crews, vehicles, equipment and settings in step between the cloud and the local database. The map comes from your own tile server, and the printer is on the LAN anyway.

Level 3

Paper is in charge

The state of the board goes to the thermal printer every few minutes, the situation sheet at the push of a button. If everything fails, the last sheet becomes the board. On restart, one person transfers what was done by hand back into the app.

From the alarm to the report

One event, one board.

This is what a storm night looks like in the command post – what happens on the board ends up in the incident report afterwards.

Alarm received

The reports come in.

Through the reporting form, by telephone, or over the open interface from the dispatch system (Divera, for example). The duty officer takes one over with a click – the report becomes an incident on the board.

21:19

Deployment

Crew and vehicles are assigned.

Personnel, vehicles and equipment onto the incident by drag and drop. The system helps the incident commander spot and surface double bookings, missing resources and contradictions. Whoever arrives checks themselves in with a QR code.

21:26

Map

Who is where.

Every incident on one map, with the vehicles optionally by GPS position. When a vehicle reaches the scene, the board proposes the status change itself.

21:48

Recon

The recon team reports back.

The recon team fills in the form on their own phone – photos included. The report hangs on the right incident instead of getting lost in a group chat.

23:30

Board

Everyone sees the same board.

The projector in the command post, the tablet on the table and the quartermaster's phone all show the same state – without anybody pressing «refresh».

06:40

Report

The incident report writes itself along the way.

The incident log and the response times build up as you go and turn into a PDF. Corrections are made with new entries, not with an eraser – so the night stays on the record.

Scope

What is in it.

Grown out of real events and exercises, not out of a feature list. Everything that comes from outside – alerting, GPS, printers – is optional: KP Rück also runs entirely without those connections.

Board & deployment

  • Columns are incident phases – cards move left to right by drag and drop
  • Assign personnel, vehicles and equipment, with a warning on double booking
  • Equipment left on scene – which resource stands at which incident, and since when
  • Priorities, running times and deadlines visible per incident
  • Assignments: several incidents as one route for a crew, order optimised by distance at the push of a button
  • QR check-in – whoever arrives checks themselves in
  • Projector view for the wall in the command post – the same incident cards as on the board, only without controls
  • Command palette and keyboard shortcuts for the fast hand

Alarms & reports

  • Open interface – any dispatch system can open incidents
  • Can take alarms from Divera optional
  • Reporting form by link or QR for telephone callers and walk-ins
  • Arrivals land in the pool, taken over with one click
  • Alerting the crew from the board itself optional
  • Recon reports with photos, straight from the recon team's own phone

Map & vehicles

  • Every incident by priority and status on one map
  • Vehicle positions by GPS optional
  • A status proposal as soon as a vehicle reaches the scene
  • Assignments as a line – which vehicle belongs to which incident
  • Map tiles from your own server at the station as well

Afterwards & operations

  • Incident report as a PDF with the incident log and the response times
  • A running log that is only ever added to, never overwritten
  • Outstanding reports as a running list – which closed incident still has none
  • Your own administration area per brigade: crew, fleet, equipment lists, incident types and priorities
  • Import and export as Excel
  • Thermal printing at the station through your own print agent optional
  • Roles: edit or read along only, dark interface for the darkened command post
  • Exercise mode with a generator and simulated journeys, cleanly separated from the real thing

Questions & answers

The most common questions.

Why switch?

No more double bookings. No report lost in a group chat. Less radio traffic for questions that are on the board anyway. Everybody sees the same picture at the same time – and the incident report is already there when you get back, instead of the weekend after.

Who owns the data?

You do. Every fire brigade runs its own KP Rück – on its own machine at the station or in the cloud. You decide where incident data, crew and fleet live; nothing goes to a vendor or to third parties.

What does it cost?

Nothing. KP Rück is open source (AGPL) – no licence, no subscription, no per-device fee. What remains is your own running costs: a small server, or a machine at the station.

What does it take to run?

Less than most people assume. A small mini-PC or server at the station is enough, and setting it up takes a few hours. After that the system largely runs by itself, and updates are one command. You need somebody to set it up once – in the brigade, at the municipality, or a friendly IT contact. And if you have nobody: get in touch, we will help with the setup.

Can we adapt it to how we work?

Yes. Every brigade has its own administration area: incident types, priorities, crew, fleet and equipment lists are all configurable there, and import and export run through Excel. And because the whole code is on GitHub , everything else can be built – by you or to order.

How do we start?

With the demo, without an account – pick a role and go. Then try it on an exercise; exercise mode is cleanly separated from the real thing. And if it fits: get in touch, we will help with the setup.
