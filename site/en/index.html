The benchmark

«When the network is gone, paper is in charge»

A board that goes black in a power cut is not a command tool. So redundancy is built in at several levels – every level works without the one above it.

Level 1

Several devices on the same state

Tablets, projector displays and phones all hang live off the same board. If one device fails or freezes, another carries on – there is nothing to rescue and nothing to catch up on.

Level 2

A second instance at the station

KP Rück can also be run locally alongside. The built-in sync keeps events, incidents, crews, vehicles, equipment and settings in step between the cloud and the local database. The map comes from your own tile server, and the printer is on the LAN anyway.

Level 3

Paper is in charge

The state of the board goes to the thermal printer every few minutes, the situation sheet at the push of a button. If everything fails, the last sheet becomes the board. On restart, one person transfers what was done by hand back into the app.

This is what a storm night looks like in the command post – what happens on the board ends up in the incident report afterwards.

Alarm received

The reports come in.

Through the reporting form, by telephone, or over the open interface from the dispatch system (Divera or FireHub, for example). The duty officer takes one over with a click – the report becomes an incident on the board. If someone reports the same incident site a second time, the board asks instead of making a second card.

Deployment

Crew and vehicles are assigned.

Personnel, vehicles and equipment onto the incident by drag and drop – or typed: «14 tlf meier» in the command palette. KP Rück detects double bookings and missing resources. The incident command decides: reassign, add, or knowingly dispatch anyway. Whoever arrives checks themselves in with a QR code.

Map

Who is where.

Every incident on one map, with the vehicles optionally by GPS position. When a vehicle reaches the scene, the board proposes the status change itself.

Recon

The recon team reports back.

The recon team fills in the form on their own phone – photos included. The report hangs on the right incident instead of getting lost in a group chat.

Board

The projector in the command post, the tablet on the table and the quartermaster's phone all show the same state – without anybody pressing «refresh».

Scope

What is in it.

Grown out of real events and exercises, not out of a feature list. Everything that comes from outside – alerting, GPS, printers – is optional: KP Rück also runs entirely without those connections.

Board & deployment

  • Columns are incident phases – cards move left to right by drag and drop
  • Assign personnel, vehicles and equipment – KP Rück warns about double assignments and makes resource conflicts visible
  • Equipment at the scene: which resource is assigned to which incident, and since when
  • Priorities, running times and deadlines visible per incident
  • Assignments: several incidents as one route for a crew, order optimised by distance at the push of a button
  • Recurring assignments, such as cordoning off an area or clearing storm-damaged trees, can be prepared and made available directly when an event is opened
  • QR check-in – whoever arrives checks themselves in
  • Duty times: who has been here since when, how much of it on an incident and who is on a break – long shifts stand out
  • Assignment slip with QR code for teams and drivers, issued directly at the command post
  • Projector view for the wall in the command post – the same incident cards as on the board, only without controls
  • Assign by typing: «14 tlf meier» in the command palette puts the TLF and Meier on incident 14 – plus keyboard shortcuts for the fast hand

Alarms & reports

  • Open interface – any dispatch system can open incidents
  • Can take alarms from Divera or FireHub optional
  • Reporting form by link or QR for telephone callers and walk-ins
  • Arrivals land in the pool, taken over with one click
  • Duplicate reports for the same incident site are detected; two incidents can be merged – crew, recon and report included – and split again
  • Alerting the crew from the board itself optional
  • Responding: who said yes to the Divera alarm shows in the roll call – checked in with one click optional
  • Recon reports with photos, straight from the recon team's phone
  • Field view for teams: see their own incident sites, report arrival and completion, request pickup, and send updates and reports with photos to the command post
  • Requests from the field – equipment, reinforcements, pickup – stay open on the incident until the command post has handled them; the crew sees where they stand on their phone

Map & vehicles

  • Every incident by priority and status on one map
  • Vehicle positions by GPS optional
  • A status proposal as soon as a vehicle reaches the scene
  • Assignments as a line – which vehicle belongs to which incident
  • Precipitation radar for the last hour and official warnings (MeteoSwiss, Alertswiss) on the map optional
  • Map tiles from your own server at the station as well

Afterwards & operations

  • Incident report as a PDF with the incident log and the response times – it builds up during the incident and is already largely prepared when the crews return
  • Incident log on the board: records status changes, assignments and messages by itself, your own entries with ⇧J – added to, never overwritten
  • Key figures while the event is still running: open reports per priority and the response times – in an exercise, as the exercise debrief
  • Outstanding-items list: open reports, pending pickups and equipment still left at an incident site remain visible until completion
  • Your own administration area per brigade: crew, fleet, equipment lists, incident types and priorities
  • Import and export as Excel
  • Thermal printing at the station through your own print agent optional
  • Roles: edit or read along only, dark interface for the darkened command post
  • Exercise mode with a generator and simulated journeys, cleanly separated from the real thing

Questions & answers

The most common questions.

Why switch?

No more double bookings. No report lost in a group chat. Less radio traffic for questions that are on the board anyway. Everybody sees the same picture at the same time – and the incident report is already there when you get back, instead of the weekend after.

Is KP Rück only for militia fire brigades?

No. KP Rück grew out of the day-to-day work of a Swiss militia fire brigade, but it suits professional fire services, industrial fire brigades and other emergency organisations just as well. Wherever resources, vehicles, equipment and crews have to be coordinated behind the lines during major incidents or simultaneous storm callouts, KP Rück plays to its strengths.

Where does the data live, and which interfaces are there?

Like KP Front, KP Rück is entirely self-hosted – on a machine at the station or in the organisation’s own cloud. Incidents, crews, vehicles and equipment live in its own database; there is no licence check and no usage tracking. External services such as online maps or address search receive only what the feature in use needs, and can be switched off. You can connect alerting systems (any dispatch system through the open interface, Divera and FireHub directly – with Divera, crew alerting too), a thermal printer on the local network and vehicle GPS positions. Every integration is optional.

What does it cost?

The software itself costs nothing. KP Rück is open source (AGPL) – no licence, no subscription, no per-device fee. What remains are your own costs for operation, infrastructure and any external support you may need.

What does it take to run?

A small mini-PC or server at the station is enough. Setup, updates and backups require someone with Docker and command-line experience, whether in the brigade, the municipality or an IT support provider. The guide and administration area lead through the setup. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.

Does KP Rück require KP Front?

No. KP Rück runs entirely on its own – with its own database, its own login and its own updates. It comes into its own alongside KP Front, the application for the front line at the incident: both take the same alarms from the dispatch system, read the same roster and classify alarms by the same keyword list, optionally with the same Divera access. Front and rear thus work from the same source data, without maintaining the alarm integration and the crew twice.

What is the local station printer for in KP Rück?

The station printer puts what matters on paper, right at the station or in the command post. KP Rück prints assignment slips for teams and drivers automatically if you wish, as soon as an incident is dispatched for the first time, or by hand at any time; the situation overview with incidents, vehicle status and crews, and the pickup list of equipment still on site, at the push of a button. As a fallback, the board can also be printed at regular intervals. What you connect is an ordinary 80 mm thermal printer (ESC/POS) on the local network – it works for any fire brigade, not just in Switzerland.

Can we adapt it to how we work?

Yes. Every brigade has its own administration area: incident types, priorities, crew, fleet and equipment lists are all configurable there, and import and export run through Excel. And because the whole code is on GitHub , everything else can be built – by you or to order.

How do we start?

With the demo, without an account – pick a role and go. Then try it on an exercise; exercise mode is cleanly separated from the real thing. If it fits, the setup guide leads through installation and operation. We are happy to answer general questions; data and operation remain the responsibility of each fire brigade.
